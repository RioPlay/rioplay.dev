[.lead]
The day language stopped being something the model re-derives on every query and became a graph I compute once and look up. The log of building that lexical substrate: the whole dictionary brings the wrong senses, so I let the corpus redefine the relationships from real usage.

== The bet (why I'm pushing this)

I'll put my name on this one directly, because it's the thesis the rest of
aden has been quietly building toward and today is the day it came into focus.

I'm Ernest Hamblen, RioPlay. The idea I keep circling: *today's AI spends most
of its money and compute re-deriving things it already worked out a moment ago.*
Every call re-embeds the same text, re-retrieves the same context, re-infers the
same relationships between the same words. It does this stochastically, on a GPU,
billed per token. That re-derivation is a treadmill, and I want aden to step off it.

The bet is simple to state. The relationships *within* language (what words
mean, how they connect, what overlaps with what) are mostly **stable**. They do
not need a billion-parameter model re-run on every query to rediscover that
`cluster` is about grouping or that `merge` and `combine` are cousins. They can be
computed **once**, into a graph: deterministic, auditable, owned, CPU-resident,
permanent. After that, a query is a *traversal*, not an *inference*. The model
gets called only for the one thing it is irreducibly good at, generating and
reasoning over something genuinely new, and even then with tight, pre-assembled
context.

I am not trying to replace the model. I'm trying to **invert AI's cost
structure**: move the durable value into a graph that's cheap and owned, and
demote the model to a thin, occasional, swappable commodity you touch as little as
possible. Make AI cheap by doing far *less* of it. If a computer can understand
language by *looking up* a structure instead of *re-thinking it from scratch*
every time, it becomes more useful to more people at a fraction of the cost. That
is the agenda, and that is why I keep pushing on it. The vision here is mine:
language as a queryable substrate, the model demoted to a commodity. I want that
on the record.

== How we got here (today's path)

The day started narrow and ended at the substrate. The arc, in order, because the
*how* matters as much as the conclusion:

. *Vocab-mismatch evals.* Confirmation gate over the **real** gen'd store (3108
  symbol cards, not toy fixtures). Result: BM25 `0/12`, hand-authored synonym
  oracle `7/12`, hybrid (BM25+dense) `0/12`. A large synonym-bridge headroom that
  neither keyword nor dense retrieval captured at R@1. (Held honestly as
  *uncalibrated*: oracle is an upper bound; hybrid-0 may be an RRF artifact.)
. *The dictionary.* Pulled Open English WordNet 2025 (OEWN, CC-BY-4.0) and mapped
  its explicit pointers onto aden's `EdgeType`s: synset→`SynonymOf`,
  hypernym→`IsA`, meronym→`PartOf`. 33 code-seed words → 567 typed triples,
  deterministic, no gloss-guessing.
. *The WSD wall, on real data.* Unscoped import is a *mix*: genuine bridges
  (`store→keep`, `search→lookup`) and wrong-sense noise (`edge→sharpness`,
  `vector→virus`, `secret→arcanum`). Gonzalo's 1998 penalty, reproduced on aden's
  own vocabulary.
. *Corpus-grounding.* Keep an edge only if both endpoints are real corpus tokens,
  tokenized by aden's own tokenizer. `567 → 296` kept / `271` dropped. The
  wrong-sense cluster filtered deterministically, no model.
. *The pivot.* The instinct that reframed everything: *don't take the dictionary's
  word for it.* Let the graph **redefine** relationships from how terms actually
  connect in the corpus: descriptive (distributional) semantics over OEWN's
  prescriptive (lexicographic) ones. PPMI over the term↔card graph,
  model-free. The contrast was stark: OEWN says `cluster→flock/clump`, the graph
  says `cluster→louvain/modular`; OEWN says `vector→virus`, the graph says
  `vector→re-embed/embeddingprovider/f32`. Domain truth, derived from counting.
. *The correction.* Grounding had been too aggressive. It deleted edges to words
  not in the codebase, but *a user's query uses words the codebase doesn't*.
  `amalgamate`, `credential`: the lexicon is the **on-ramp from arbitrary English
  into the domain**, so you want all of it. Grounding is a *ranking* signal, not an
  *ingest* filter.
. *The substrate.* So: take the ENTIRE dictionary. The full OEWN as a graph is
  **172,018 edges** (77,575 `SynonymOf`, 87,978 `IsA`, 6,465 `PartOf`) across
  **69,150 lemma nodes**, generated in **2.4 seconds**, 16 MB on disk, and that's
  a floor (antonyms, inverses, derivations still to come). The whole of English
  fits in a rounding error of compute.

== The honest boundary

For the published record, the precise claim is the strong one, so I'll draw the
line myself: aden is a **retriever and knowledge substrate, not a reasoner.** It
will never write the Louvain implementation or reason about a case it's never
seen. That stays the model's job. What aden does is make that the *only* thing
you pay neural compute for. Three constraints we're holding to:

* *The lexicon is generic → it lives once, as a shared overlay,* not copied into
  every project's `.aden/store`.
* *Storing it all ≠ better retrieval automatically.* The full graph is necessary
  but not sufficient; traversal-time sense ranking is the remaining hard part.
* *Word-sense disambiguation moves from ingest-time (hard) to traversal-time
  (tractable, gated by domain context).* Ingest everything; rank smart.

== The retrieval redo, where the afternoon went

The lexicon got built for real: a sign-off'd write to a *dedicated* store, never
`.aden/store`. The full OEWN is now ~436k symmetric edges at `~/.cache/aden/lexicon`,
queryable and reversible. Then the real question: does any of it make *retrieval*
better? The answer was a string of honest negatives that resolved into one real win.

. *Query expansion is dead, five ways.* Expanding a search query with a word's
  relations (OEWN naive, OEWN corpus-grounded, corpus-PPMI bulk, corpus-PPMI
  targeted) lifts routing `+1/12` at best, vs a hand-authored oracle's `7/12`.
  Polysemy floods; the domain-specific bridges OEWN simply lacks. Expansion is the
  wrong mechanism, but I had over-claimed "relations don't work" from it.
. *R@1 was hiding the whole iceberg.* Every gate had scored top-1 hit/miss. Once we
  measured *rank-of-gold* at R@5/R@10/MRR (and turned on the dense path), the signal
  appeared: dense pulls the gold from rank ~200 to rank ~10, and the oracle proves the
  benchmark is *solvable* (MRR 0.68). Retrieval's problem was never "can't find it";
  it's "gets it to rank ~10, not the top."
. *A shipped-path flaw fell out.* Pure dense beats the shipped hybrid RRF
  (MRR 0.146 vs 0.061). Equal-weight fusion *drags dense down* by averaging in
  BM25's near-random ranks. A weight sweep found dense-weighted RRF (1:5) at
  MRR 0.182, ~3× the shipped fusion, recovering a top-1. Fix = adaptive/dense-weighted
  fusion (production change, validate-then-sign-off).
. *The first real win for relationships.* Used as a *reranker* on dense's top-20
  (not an expander), corpus relationships (PPMI) lift R@5 `3→5` and MRR `0.146→0.184`,
  the first non-oracle top-1. The mechanism was the problem all along, not the relations.
. *And the layering holds.* Layer 2 adds an OEWN SynonymOf *agreement* signal
  (a relationship the dictionary AND the corpus both attest), which nudges it to MRR `0.198`,
  R@5 `4`. The progression is consistent: dense `0.146` → +PPMI `0.184` → +OEWN `0.198`,
  every validated layer positive. Increments diminish (+0.038, +0.014) and the oracle
  (`0.678`) is still far on 12 probes. Not cracked, but moving the right way, methodically.
Layer 3 (crate-domain scoping) then came in *flat* (`0.197`). The progression converges at
L2: the rerank lever is real (`+35%` MRR over the dense baseline) but bounded. The plateau the
methodical loop predicts. And the two wins don't *compound*. Reranking the fusion-fixed base
(`0.195`) is *worse* than reranking pure dense (`0.203`): both inject the same signal dense
lacks, so it's pick-one, not stack. The complete automatic map: dense `0.146` → fusion `0.182`
→ rerank `0.203`, against the oracle's `0.678`. So ~`0.20` is the *automatic ceiling* on this
benchmark; the gap to the oracle is *domain knowledge* (this codebase uses Louvain) that no
generic method derives.

And a negative worth recording: *naive whitening hurts.* Mean-centering / diagonal whitening
of the bge vectors dropped MRR `0.145 → 0.110`, because bge is a *cosine-trained sentence model*,
not raw BERT: globally reshaping its geometry destroys learned structure rather than freeing it.
The dense *base* is good as-is; the iterative whiten↔rerank loop loses its whitening engine
unless the *graph-regularized* form (gentle, structure-preserving) threads the needle, which is
uncertain. The working levers stay the *rerank layers* and the *fusion fix*.

The lesson worth keeping: *measure R@5/MRR, not R@1.* A binary top-1 metric scored a
massive rank-shift as a flat "miss" and nearly buried a real result.

== The population layer, when the graph writes its own relationships

The afternoon's levers all *consumed* a graph; the evening's move was to *populate*
one, the bet from the top of this entry made concrete.

First, the honest dead-end that pointed the way. I tried routing `NL → prose → code`
along aden's *existing* edges: dense-match the prose, then spread relevance down the
`Mentions`/`Demonstrates` links to the symbol the prose describes. It came in flat.
Those edges only link unambiguous names, so they're sparse by construction and the
boosts rarely fired. The lesson wasn't "routing doesn't work." It was: *the graph
isn't dense enough yet.* Don't write a cleverer router, write a richer graph.

So I built the thing this entry has been circling: a **concept graph** where the
nodes are parts of speech and the edges are relationships the *model* draws from how
words are actually used here. Each distinctive corpus term becomes a POS-tagged node
(POS from OEWN; pure-code identifiers tagged `?`) carrying a **context-centroid**:
the bge embedding averaged over every card the term appears in. That centroid *is*
the term's sense **in this corpus**, so the relationships come out sense-correct by
construction. Mutual-kNN over those centroids gives the `SimilarTo` overlap edges.
1,882 nodes, 1,677 mutual edges, one pass, written to a JSON artifact. The store
never touched.

And the relationships are *right*. The WSD wall the generic dictionary couldn't
clear, cleared by grounding meaning in usage:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
|Term |Generic dictionary (OEWN) |Concept graph (this corpus)

|`cluster` |`flock`, `clump`    |`community`, `modular`, `connect`
|`fuse`    |`weld`, `melt`      |`rrf`, `fusion`, `reciprocal`, `bm25`
|`secret`  |`arcanum`, `occult` |`credential`, `pem`, `secure`
|`vector`  |`virus`             |`embeddingprovider`, `embed`, `bge`
|===

Then the real test: does the populated graph make *retrieval* better? The answer
split cleanly along *how you consume it*, and that distinction is the whole point:

* *Expansion still dies*, even with perfect relations. Adding a term's concept
  neighbours to the query and re-embedding dropped MRR `0.141 → 0.074`. The sixth
  independent confirmation that expansion is the wrong mechanism: it *drifts* the
  query embedding (and feeds it broken stems) instead of sharpening it.
* *Reranking wins.* The *same* relations, used to boost the dense top-20 candidates
  that actually **contain** a query term's concept-neighbours (no embedding drift,
  just a lexical-overlap signal added to the score), lifted MRR `0.141 → 0.241`,
  *+71%*. That beats the afternoon's best automatic reranker (PPMI+OEWN at `0.203`):
  the strongest automatic signal yet on this benchmark, and proof that the *quality
  of the relationships* (sense-correct, corpus-grounded) is what moved the number.

Then I swept the knobs, to be sure `0.241` wasn't a lucky point. It wasn't the
ceiling either. Tightening to fewer, higher-precision neighbours (`k=4`) and reranking
the dense top-20 lifts it to **`0.289`** (+105% over dense, R@1 `1 → 3`). Two structural
facts fell out, both clean: the boost weight `λ` is *irrelevant* (`0.25 = 0.5 = 1 = 2`
all tie, because the rerank is really a *lexicographic sort by overlap-count, then dense-score*),
and *precision beats recall* in the neighbour set, since more neighbours just match the wrong
cards. The honest boundary still stands: twelve probes, and the oracle at `0.641` is
domain knowledge no generic method derives. But the *shape* of the win is the claim:
*populate the graph with model-drawn, sense-correct relationships, then consume them by
reranking, not expanding.* The substrate redefined its own edges from usage, and consumed
the right way, beat every generic method we'd tried. The bet paying a first dividend.

== What came next

. *Validate `0.289` beyond 12 probes*: the sweep locked the knobs (`k=4`, `head=20`, `λ`
  insensitive); the open risk is the thin probe set, not the tuning. Author more
  synonym-mismatch probes and/or validate on external eval corpora before calling `0.289`
  the settled ceiling.
. *Ship the fusion fix*: dense-weighted / adaptive RRF, validated on real eval corpora,
  then into `rrf_fuse` on sign-off. Still the one concrete, user-facing win that's
  independent of the rerank layer.
. *Typed edges, now grounded*: a research pass over `the research notes` confirms the
  Tier-1 set (`SynonymOf`/`IsA`/`PartOf`/`AntonymOf`) is *already* first-class in
  `aden-core`; the gap is the bulk *producer*, not the schema. The synthesis to test next:
  gate OEWN's typed edges by the concept graph's context-centroid proximity, making them
  *typed and sense-correct at once*, which is exactly the graph-context-gated WSD this codebase
  has been hypothesizing. POS-gating and the overloaded `\` pointer are the known hazards.
